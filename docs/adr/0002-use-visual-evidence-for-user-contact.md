# Use visual evidence for user contact selection

User contact frames and their confidence are selected from visual ball-and-racket evidence rather than acoustic peak timing. Sound propagation and recording synchronization can displace an audio event from the corresponding video image, while comparison requires the closest available visual representation of contact. Audio locates broad candidate regions, but only visual evidence from a frame and its neighbors scores the discrete source frames. The detector does not invent an interpolated contact timestamp: the strongest existing frame is selected, ties resolve toward the earlier frame, and a region without sufficiently strong evidence is omitted.

This refinement belongs to `tennis-compare`; the legacy `tennis-cut` workflow retains its audio-derived timing. A user-video region that cannot support a contact-frame decision is omitted: it never falls back to audio timing or manual selection. During staged shot-type rollout, comparison requests for an unsupported pro shot type fail clearly rather than falling back to audio alignment. A pro-video contact frame may be supplied manually because a pro video contains one known swing and is reusable across comparisons.

The accepted side-view forehand prototype uses stock YOLO detections to build body-relative racket-and-ball position, visibility, quality, and motion features across real source frames. An 85/15 blend of an ordinary HistGradientBoosting ranker and a family-balanced ranker directly selects the strongest discrete frame. The earlier deterministic scorer remains diagnostic evidence and enforces only hard prerequisites: decoded frames, a player-related racket, and moving-ball evidence. It no longer chooses the production frame or corroborates the learned ranker.

The accepted ensemble is exported as a portable JSON package resource and installed with `tennis-cut`. `tennis-compare` resolves and validates that internal resource automatically; it does not expose ranker paths or training controls to users. Decisions, including omissions and diagnostic evidence, are cached by source fingerprint, candidate timestamp, search radius, and detector identity.

The reviewed benchmark contains 300 contacts split by recording family: 164 training, 38 validation, 35 test, and 63 out-of-scope-view contacts. Across the 69 resolved validation and test contacts, the ensemble selected an acceptable visual state or one adjacent frame in every case; 58 were exact and 11 used the allowed tolerance. The motivating Ruud pro clip selects frame 636, adjacent to the visually acceptable 635–636 contact state. Only four unresolved contacts landed in validation and test, so this evidence does not justify a separately learned localizability gate. Until naturally occurring failures supply enough negative examples, omission remains conservative and evidence-based rather than claiming a statistically validated abstention model.
